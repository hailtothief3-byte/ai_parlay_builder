# Buyer Demo Walkthrough

## Purpose

This guide is for a future buyer, collaborator, or operator who needs to understand AI Parlay Builder quickly without wandering through every surface in the app.

The goal is not to show every feature. The goal is to show that the product is:

- real
- differentiated
- usable
- handoff-friendly

## Best demo posture

For the cleanest first impression:

1. use `Core` or `Pro` product plan
2. use `Simple` view
3. start on `NBA`
4. use the board that currently has the calmest live or demo state

If live-provider data is noisy, use the app's demo-backed workflows instead of forcing a fragile live demo.

## Recommended 3 to 5 minute flow

### 1. Start on Overview

Use `Overview` to frame the product.

What to say:

`This is a workflow and intelligence platform for prop research, ticket construction, DFS handoff, and post-result learning.`

What to point out:

- current operating posture
- next-step coaching
- workflow status
- notification center

Why this matters:

- it makes the app feel guided instead of chaotic
- it shows the product is more than a raw odds table

### 2. Open Edge Scanner

Use `Edge Scanner` to show the ranking layer.

What to say:

`This is where the app turns the board into prioritized candidates instead of asking the operator to scan everything manually.`

What to point out:

- ranked candidates
- confidence / edge support
- filtering controls
- smart-score context

Why this matters:

- it shows the intelligence layer is inspectable
- it gives the buyer a clear value story around speed and decision support

### 3. Open Parlay Lab

Use `Parlay Lab` to show the build layer.

What to say:

`This is the construction workflow. The app can turn ranked candidates into a cleaner sportsbook or DFS ticket-building process.`

What to point out:

- smart parlay profile
- recommended leg count
- stake planning
- live/demo slip packaging
- DFS handoff packaging when relevant

Why this matters:

- it connects ranking to action
- it shows this is a workflow product, not just a dashboard

### 4. Open Results & Grading

Use `Results & Grading` to show the memory and review loop.

What to say:

`The product is designed to remember what was built, review what happened, and turn that history into better future decisions.`

What to point out:

- saved tickets
- tracked picks
- settlement queue
- bankroll journal
- ticket review insights

Why this matters:

- it proves the product has retention and learning value
- it makes the app easier to position as an asset instead of a one-screen tool

### 5. Mention Pro depth without lingering there

If the buyer wants more depth, switch briefly into `Pro` view.

What to say:

`The deeper audit and review layers are there, but the product can be demoed in a calmer surface first and opened up later for tuning.`

What to point out briefly:

- audit breakdowns
- smart leg review
- backtest / calibration surfaces
- owner documentation posture

Why this matters:

- it shows depth without overwhelming the buyer

## What not to over-explain

Avoid spending too long on:

- raw provider configuration
- every tab in one sitting
- edge-case DFS adapter caveats
- legacy file naming in the repo
- low-level implementation details unless the buyer is technical

The buyer usually needs product clarity before codebase detail.

## Suggested demo script

Use this short script if you want a simple buyer-facing walkthrough:

`AI Parlay Builder is a workflow and intelligence platform for prop research, ticket construction, DFS handoff, and post-result learning.`

`The first thing to notice is that the app is guided. Overview gives the operator posture, workflow status, and next-step coaching instead of dropping them into a raw board.`

`Edge Scanner ranks candidates so the operator can focus on better spots faster. Parlay Lab turns that into a structured build workflow for sportsbook or DFS packaging.`

`Results and Grading closes the loop by saving tickets, reviewing outcomes, and giving the product memory over time.`

`The deeper review and audit layers are still there in Pro mode, but the surface can stay calm for first-run users, demos, or future buyers.`

## Strongest positioning points

When the buyer asks why this is valuable, emphasize:

1. it combines `scan`, `build`, `track`, and `learn` in one product
2. it supports both live and demo-backed workflows
3. it includes packaging for sportsbook and DFS use cases
4. it has inspectable intelligence instead of black-box recommendations
5. it already has buyer/operator handoff documentation

## If the live board is noisy

Use this framing:

`The app can still be demonstrated cleanly in demo-backed mode, which is useful because it reduces dependence on provider availability during a handoff or product review.`

That keeps the demo stable without sounding defensive.
